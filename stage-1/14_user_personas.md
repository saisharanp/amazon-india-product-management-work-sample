# Stage 1 — Amazon India User Personas

These are research-informed provisional personas based on the 22-record public sample. They are hypotheses for validation, not demographic claims.

## Persona 1 — Trust-sensitive exception resolver

**Profile:** A frequent or high-value shopper who is comfortable using Amazon but becomes highly dissatisfied when delivery, refund, or return states are unclear.

**Jobs to be done**

- Know whether the order is actually on track.
- Reach the right support owner without repeating the story.
- Recover money or a replacement predictably when the order fails.

**Pain points**

- “Delivered” status does not match reality.
- Delivery contact or handoff evidence is unavailable.
- Different agents provide different next steps.
- Refund status is not the same as money received.

**Success looks like**

- One accurate timeline.
- A visible owner and deadline.
- A clear recovery choice: deliver, replace, or refund.

**Opportunity implication:** Highest-priority persona for the proposed post-purchase exception problem.

## Persona 2 — Intent-driven comparison shopper

**Profile:** A shopper who uses search, sort, filters, ratings, and price comparisons to reduce decision effort.

**Jobs to be done**

- Narrow a large catalog quickly.
- Trust that sorting and filters are actually applied.
- Compare products using relevant attributes.

**Pain points**

- Sort/filter results appear inconsistent.
- UX changes or recommendations can feel distracting.
- Product review and seller signals may not resolve uncertainty.

**Success looks like**

- Stable filter state.
- Transparent result ordering.
- Fast comparison of relevant attributes.

**Opportunity implication:** Backup opportunity if discovery issues appear more prevalent after larger sampling.

## Persona 3 — Convenience-led repeat buyer

**Profile:** A customer who values Amazon’s breadth, payment options, delivery convenience, and routine repeat purchasing.

**Jobs to be done**

- Reorder familiar products quickly.
- Use preferred payment or COD.
- Track delivery without active intervention.

**Pain points**

- Reliability failures are more frustrating because convenience is the reason for returning.
- Prime value feels weaker when delivery promises fail.
- Order/account issues can interrupt repeat behavior.

**Success looks like**

- Predictable fulfilment.
- Low-effort support.
- Clear benefits for membership and repeat use.

**Opportunity implication:** Important retention segment for post-launch measurement.

## Persona 4 — Time-critical or bulk purchaser

**Profile:** A customer buying multiple units for an event, household need, or deadline.

**Jobs to be done**

- Confirm quantity and arrival date before committing.
- Know quickly if supply or delivery risk changes.
- Escalate before the deadline is missed.

**Pain points**

- Quantity mismatch.
- Late cancellation after reassurance.
- No high-severity escalation path.

**Success looks like**

- Quantity and availability confirmation.
- Deadline-aware delivery confidence.
- Immediate recovery options.

**Opportunity implication:** Critical edge case for launch validation and service blueprinting.

## Persona prioritization

| Persona | Evidence confidence | Problem severity | Strategic priority |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Trust-sensitive exception resolver | High | Critical | Primary |
| Convenience-led repeat buyer | Medium | High | Primary retention lens |
| Intent-driven comparison shopper | Medium | High | Backup opportunity |
| Time-critical or bulk purchaser | Medium | Critical | Edge-case validation |
